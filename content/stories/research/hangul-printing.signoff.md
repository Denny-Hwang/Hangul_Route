## Remaining uncertainty
1. ㄴ wording: the Haerye's 上腭 literally means upper palate or jaw; the standard Korean rendering (encykorea) is "윗잇몸" and the story follows that. No finer anatomy is claimed.
2. Hangeul Day: the actual calendar behind Oct 9 is not established. The story says only "by the method they used".
3. Panjeon: current entry rules are time-dependent (UNESCO restriction vs Haeinsa's pre-booked tours since 2021).
4. Tripitaka lacquer: 한국민족문화대백과사전 and the 국가유산포털 MoW page say lacquer preserves the carving; 박상진 doubts its protective effect. The narration keeps "to protect them" as the official wording.
5. Tripitaka block count: 81,258 comes from a survey under Japanese rule and includes some Joseon re-carved blocks. The story says only "more than 80,000".
6. Jikji disciple 달담 vs 달잠: not named in the story.
7. The 1443 date: the Sillok entry falls in the 12th lunar month of Sejong 25; all cited sources say 1443.
8. unesco.org/en/prizes/literacy was not reachable. The claim is otherwise backed by the MCST release.
