# Picture triage (2026-11-17)

Judged by eye from the sheets in `sheets/` (see `index.md`), mainly at the 64 px and 54 px renders. One reviewer, no
second opinion, no learner test: treat scores as a first pass. Score = five criteria, 0 to 2 each, written `s/f/l/c/a`:
**s** silhouette names it at 64 px, **f** one defining feature, **l** no look-alike in its set, **c** not colour-only,
**a** culturally accurate. Verdict: keep 8+, tweak 5 to 7, redraw 4 or less.

## Conventions actually used, and rule conflicts

### WordArt (reference; not re-reviewed)
F-VOC-002 §3.5 and `design/brief/illustrations/word-art.md`: 100-unit grid; ink outline `text.primary`, 3 for the
silhouette and 2 for details, never thinner than 2 (test C3); at most 4 fills (C4); subject 60 to 70 % of the frame,
tested (C2); no text (C10); readable at 48 px and in silhouette (C9); own `colors.vocab` group with a true red.

### Heritage cards, Stage 1
- Grid 200, so one unit is half a WordArt unit. Stroke widths in the file: 2 (62 uses), 1.5 (36), 2.5 (14), 3 (12),
  1 (7). The typical outline is 2/200 = 0.64 px at 64 px; WordArt's is 3/100 = 1.9 px. About three times thinner.
- Outline colour: `brand.primaryDark` (46) and `text.secondary` (41); `text.primary` only 14. Mid browns, not ink.
- Fills: `feedback.nudge` amber (25), `surface.canvas` cream (21), `surface.paper` (13): cream objects on a cream
  frame (hanji, ink, yutnori, sebae, moon, family-table bowls).
- An 8 % theme-tint rounded square is part of the art. Subject size is untested; several subjects are thin or small
  (chopsticks, yutnori, gayageum, sea).
- No test looks at the drawing: only "there is a case per id".

### Heritage cards, upper stages
- Same 200 grid but heavier: 2.5 (27) and 3 (22) dominate, mostly `text.secondary`. Stage 2 and 3 objects are close
  to WordArt quality (byeol, gom, norae, bokjumeoni).
- Stage 4 uses one template (two pals plus bubbles) for five cards; Stage 7 uses one template (word tile plus a ruled
  page with a Korean sentence) for five cards; legends use a frame with the Korean name. Templates save bytes (the
  tests set byte budgets per drawing) and cost distinct silhouettes.
- Stages 6, 7 and legends draw Hangul with a font (`SvgText`); a test requires it.

### Rule conflicts
- **R1, no red.** Card brief §6 and §8: "No red except dancheong orange accents", and §4 kimchi: "use `theme.life`
  amber for warmth, NOT alarm-red". Result: kimchi is orange and amber. Scene art has the same limit: `ColorToken`
  excludes the `vocab` group and `feedback.danger`, and `brand.primary` (the only red) may be on at most 15 % of an
  item's shapes (`LIMITS.redPercent`), so the hidden-scene kimchi is `theme.letters` orange. WordArt alone may use
  `vocab.red`, and `word-art/__tests__/import-guard.test.ts` forbids `colors.vocab` anywhere else. A red kimchi needs
  one of: draw it as a WordArt glyph (`word:food-kimchi`), or amend the guard and the brief, or use `brand.primary`
  (#B24915, a brown-red) as the main fill.
- **R2, outline weight.** The brief says "~3px at 600x800", which is 1 unit on the 200 grid: written for a poster-size
  card, not for 54 to 64 px question pictures. F-VOC-002 §3.11 then reuses 13 cards at 64 px next to WordArt.
- **R3, palette.** Brief: "3 to 5 colours, all from the theme's tint family + cream" on a cream ground gives low
  contrast; WordArt's dark ink outline is what makes cream objects readable.
- **R4, the per-card briefs themselves** ask for the weak picture: sea = "a small cream sailboat ... in the center";
  sebae = "folded hands resting on a tiny silk bag"; family-table = top-down; kimchi = "3 visible cabbage layers";
  moon and chuseok both "a full warm cream moon centered"; kite = diamond, while the card's fact text names the
  shield kite; gayageum = "two small movable bridges" (a gayageum has one per string).
- **R5, text in pictures.** WordArt C10 and scene-art rule 7 forbid font text; upper cards of Stages 6 and 7 and the
  legends are identified mainly by printed Hangul, with no romanization inside the picture. If such a card is ever
  used as a question picture the answer is printed on it. (I did not check whether any is.)
- **R6, stale audience and style lines.** `heritage-card-art.md` §6 prompt: "Korean learning app for kids 5-11";
  `docs/design/illustration-style.md`: "영어권 어린이 5-11세", "Pixar-influenced", "2px stroke", Hoya with a striped
  T-shirt at 3.5 heads. None matches CLAUDE.md §1 (any age) or the shipped art.
- **R7, Hoya thinking mouth** (see §7) against `scene-art-style.md` "no frown" and the comment in `hoya.ts`.
- **R8, byte budgets shape the art.** `card-ids.test.ts`: each upper drawing at most 2,000 B (1,700 B / 1,600 B for
  later rows), module at most 34,000 B. Redrawing the template cards as real subjects will need those lines raised.

### Scene art (settings, props, hidden heritage, realia)
- `scene-art-style.md` and `rules.ts` `LIMITS`: 400x300 canvas; strokes `borderWidth` thin/base/thick = 1/2/3 units;
  prop at most 40 shapes, 3 KB, 5 colours; setting 60 shapes, 6 KB, 6 colours; scene 8 colours, 160 shapes; no text;
  faces absent on people.
- Settings use no outlines and low-opacity fills (calm backdrops, fine). Props use `border.strong` / `text.secondary`
  outlines at 2 to 3 units on boxes of 50 to 130 units, so relatively much heavier than the cards. Hidden-heritage
  things use `text.secondary` at `base` with saturated theme fills and fill their bay: this is why they read better
  than the cards of the same subject (compare hanbok, paengi, yutpan).
- Weak spot: cream figures on cream (`surface.sunken` clothes, `border.subtle`-like outline) and pale `full-moon`.
